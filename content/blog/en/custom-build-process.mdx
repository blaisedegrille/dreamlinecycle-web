A custom bicycle is not about putting more expensive parts on a frame. It is about every decision having a reason: your body, your roads and the way you like to ride.

Here is how we work, and what to expect when you come in for a build.

## 1. The conversation

The first step is not choosing a frame. It is a conversation. You do not need to speak in technical terms. It is enough to tell us:

- what you use the bike for: commuting, road, gravel, touring, MTB,
- how much and how fast you ride,
- what you ride now, and what bothers you about it,
- whether there is a part you want to keep,
- what budget you have in mind.

This conversation is useful even if you do not yet know what you want. The thinking starts here.

## 2. Size and geometry

Next comes fit. Frame size is only part of it. Saddle position, bar height, reach and the load your body can carry all matter.

If you already have a bike that feels comfortable, we can start from there. If not, we work from your measurements and the intended use. The goal is not a race geometry at any cost. It is a position you can live with on a long ride.

<Callout title="Important">
Geometry is not magic. Good geometry simply means the bike holds you where you need to be, and you are not fighting it.
</Callout>

## 3. The parts list

The parts list is not a list of the most expensive components. It is a list of parts that fit each other and fit you.

We go through the drivetrain, brakes, wheels, tyres and contact points. For every decision we explain why we chose it, and where it is safe to save money without ruining the ride.

> The best component is the one you never notice. The worst is the one you think about on every ride.

## 4. The build

The build happens in the workshop. Frame preparation, wheel lacing, drivetrain setup and brake adjustment are all hand work.

A few things worth knowing:

- wheels are laced and checked by hand,
- brakes and shifting are set up for real-world use,
- protective film goes on the critical contact points,
- a test ride and fine-tuning come before handover.

## 5. Handover and aftercare

At handover we do not just give you the bike. We go through how it works, what to watch for, and when to come back for the first check.

Warranty terms are set out in writing. We avoid vague promises, because a good relationship starts when everyone knows exactly what was agreed.

## How long does it take?

This is the hardest question, because every build is different. The answer depends on three things:

1. how custom the geometry and parts list are,
2. how long the parts take to arrive,
3. how much work is already in the workshop.

That is why we do not invent a deadline. At the end of the conversation we give you a realistic schedule, and we tell you if something slips.

## What to bring

If you have a picture of what you want, bring it. If you have a bike that works well for you, bring it. If you have a part you definitely want to use, bring it. The best builds usually start with a lot of questions.

And if you are new to all this, that is fine too. The first step is always the same: let's talk.
