The wheel is where your bike meets the road. Every vibration, every corner and every watt reaches you through the wheels first. And yet wheels are often the last thing we think about when planning a build.

## The difference is not cosmetic

A machine-built wheel can be perfectly fine. Trouble starts when the tension is uneven: a few spokes are almost loose while others are already at their limit. You may not see it from the side, but over months it fatigues the rim and the hub.

Hand building is slower, and that is the point:

- spoke length is chosen for the rim and hub,
- tension is checked in several passes,
- the wheel reaches its final tension while we watch how the rim responds,
- at the end we check not only that it is straight, but how it behaves under load.

> A straight wheel is not automatically a good wheel. A good wheel stays straight under load.

## When is a hand-built wheel worth it?

Not everyone needs a custom wheel. For a city bike doing a few thousand kilometres a year, a good quality factory wheel can be more than enough. But there are cases where hand building earns its keep:

1. **On a custom bike.** If the geometry and parts are built around you, the wheels should not be an off-the-shelf afterthought.
2. **With higher weight or load.** Spoke count, tension and rim stiffness start to matter.
3. **If you ride mixed terrain.** A gravel or touring wheel has to cope with rough surfaces and bigger tyres.
4. **When reliability matters more than grams.** Commuting, touring or long events are no place to worry about a wheel.

## What to bring

When we talk about a hand-built wheel, the important questions are simple:

- your weight and how you ride,
- the tyre and pressure you use,
- the roads you ride,
- whether you already have a hub or rim you want to use,
- which brake system you run.

From that we can judge the lacing pattern and tension that suits you. The goal is not the lightest possible wheel, but the one that fits best.

<Callout title="Tip">
A good wheel is one you forget about. If you are listening for a noise on every ride, something is still not right.
</Callout>

## Maintenance: less than you think

A well-built wheel does not need much attention. Check tension after the first few hundred kilometres, because that is when the spokes really settle. After that, once a season — or after a big impact — is usually enough.

If a wheel starts to "work", do not just tighten the loosest spoke and hope. Bad tension damages a rim faster than a small dent. Bring it in and we will take a look.

## In short

A hand-built wheel is not a luxury for everyone. But if you are building a bike you intend to keep, wheels are one of the places where attention pays off. The difference is not marketing. It is how the wheel behaves on the hundredth ride, not just the first.
