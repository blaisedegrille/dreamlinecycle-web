Winter does not have to mean the bike goes into the cellar. Prepared properly, the cold, wet months can be perfectly pleasant. And if you do put it away, a little preparation makes spring much easier.

Here is an eight-point list worth working through.

## 1. Clean, but sensibly

Winter salt and road grime are the biggest enemies. Wash the bike with lukewarm water and a mild cleaner, not a pressure washer. Do not spray directly into bearings or around the bottom bracket.

Clean the chain thoroughly and dry it before lubricating.

## 2. Chain and drivetrain

In winter the chain gets dirty faster. Wet, salty road spray mixed with grit turns into grinding paste.

- clean the chain regularly,
- use the right lubricant: heavier for wet weather, lighter for dry,
- check chain wear,
- inspect cassette and chainring wear.

<Callout title="Waxing">
Clean, dry waxing is a lovely solution, but in winter on salted roads it needs more frequent attention. If you ride wet roads often, ask us which approach suits your use.
</Callout>

## 3. Tyres

Winter tyre pressure is often lower than in summer, because cold roads and wet surfaces ask for more grip. But too little pressure increases puncture risk and rolling resistance.

Check:

- tread and sidewalls,
- pressure weekly,
- valves and valve caps,
- embedded stones or glass.

## 4. Brakes

In winter, brake pads and rotors can wear faster because of moisture and grit.

- check pad thickness,
- inspect rotor wear,
- adjust lever free play,
- listen for squealing or rubbing.

## 5. Bearings and steering

Moisture works its way into bearings. If you feel a "crunch" when turning the bars or spinning the wheels, do not ignore it.

- headset,
- hub bearings,
- bottom bracket,
- pedals.

These are not always a home job without the right tools. Bring the bike in and we will take a look.

## 6. Lights and visibility

It gets dark early in winter. Lights are not an accessory; they are essential equipment.

- front and rear light function,
- spare batteries or charging,
- reflectors,
- clean, unscratched lenses.

## 7. Mudguards and frame protection

Mudguards are not just cosmetic. They protect your clothes, the drivetrain and the frame. If protective film is fitted, check that it has not started to lift at the edges.

## 8. Storage

If the bike is resting for a longer period:

- clean and dry it,
- let a little air out of the tyres,
- apply a thin layer of oil to the chain,
- avoid a damp, unheated room,
- hang it up if possible so the weight is not on the tyres.

## When to book a service

If you are unsure about any of the above, bring it in. Winter is a good time to replace worn parts at a calm pace, rather than on the first day of the spring season.

An inspection is not a luxury. Better an hour now than a week off the bike in spring.
